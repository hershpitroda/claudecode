# Fitted: treatment

Style: Exploded Teardown. Length: 45 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. English voice and burned-in English subtitles.

## Three candidate structures

1. **One orbit, two stops (assembly in reverse).** The bare shell turns. The fit-out hangs above it in layers on one vertical axis, then lands layer by layer, with the camera stopping at the desk and the chair. It closes with people at work and dimensions drawn on the furniture.
2. **"How it works" along a person's path.** Follow one worker from the door to the desk: floor, chair, desk, screen, booth. Each part lifts out of the finished office as they pass it. This needs a character to act and a long walking path, and it hides the "empty to full" change the brief is about.
3. **Before / after, side by side.** Two floor plates, one bare and one fitted, held still while each difference is named. It reads well, but it is static. The style wants a camera that never stops travelling, and a split view breaks its "no split screens" rule.

**Chosen: 1.** The brief is a transformation, from an empty shell to a working office. An exploded stack that *descends and closes* is the teardown grammar run in reverse, so the style's native move (layers apart on one axis, then closing bottom-first) becomes the story itself. Two stops give the furniture the macro time it deserves, and one orbit keeps the camera moving.

## Logline and arc

An empty concrete office floor receives its fit-out in exploded layers (carpet, desks, chairs, screens, light), each landing in order, until people sit down and the ceiling closes over a working office.

- **Setup (0–5 s):** the bare shell turns in near-silence. Concrete, columns, glass, nothing else.
- **Turn (5–33 s):** the fit-out appears above it in layers and lands one layer at a time. A desk performs its one real motion (seated to standing). The lights switch on inside while the stack is still open.
- **Ending (33–45 s):** after a held near-silence, people take their seats and the ceiling layer lands. The camera pulls back over a closed, working floor with its ergonomic dimensions drawn on it, then the title.

## Benchmark

- **Hardware-launch exploded product films** (layers lifting on one axis, orbit timing, a long settle after a fast middle). *Learn:* stagger, easing, orbiting while things move, and labels drawn on the object. *Don't take:* products, layouts, typefaces, music.
- **Architectural fit-out axonometrics** (cutaway office models seen from a high three-quarter view, roof removed). *Learn:* reading a room by its zones from above, and a muted material palette with one accent. *Don't take:* any firm's drawings, furniture designs or branding.

## Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles are a centred strip in the bottom 12 % in Inter, with a soft shadow and no box, English only.
- Labels use a fixed structure: a two-digit index in the accent (#ff6b3d), the name in a 30 px medium sans, and one spec line in JetBrains Mono at about 60 % ink.
- Dimension text sits on a small dark plate that follows the line's angle.
- The title card holds for 4 s or more: "Fitted" in the name sans, with a mono line under it: "a model · ergonomic ranges from ANSI/BIFMA G1-2013".

## Colour and materials

- Ground: graphite sweep #34363a → #0d0e10. Ink: #f1efe9. Accent: #ff6b3d (dimension lines, label indices, and one matching detail on the product: the task chairs' seat-adjust lever).
- Shell: warm grey board-formed concrete, dark mullions, light blue-grey glass.
- Fit-out: oak desk tops, white powder-coated frames, charcoal mesh chairs (lifted to dark grey), sage felt booth, oatmeal sofa, black screens with soft-lit faces, green plants, warm pendant lights.
- People: plain matte mannequin figures in muted clothing colours. No faces and no brands.

## What is modelled, and how

There are no CAD files for this office, so everything is a model built from published dimension ranges (`FACTS.md`). The voice says it is a model. Only sourced numbers appear on screen, and the floor plate's own size is not labelled.
